01 · The thesis

Workflow understanding, not clones

A clone copies the easy 20%, the screens, and leaves you to build the hard 80% from nothing: operations, data, partners, trust. A workflow map shows where the money leaks. That leak is the product.

Don't build

"AI Hospital App"

Too broad, competes head-on with Practo, and needs a hospital's whole operation behind it.

Better

"AI for clinic operations"

Narrower, but still a platform. Too many workflows to win at once.

Build

"Lab report verification that saves 200 staff-hours a month"

One workflow, one buyer, one number. Sellable in a single conversation.

03 · Phase 0

Measure the crawler on software whose code you can read

Before trusting research on Practo or Zerodha, prove the crawler's accuracy on open-source systems you run on your own laptop. You have their code and docs, so you know the correct answer, and you get a reference backend for the sector at the same time.

The test, in five steps

Run it locally→Crawl L1–L5→Rebuild workflow maps from the crawl only→Compare with code + docs→Accuracy % per layer

The list of misses shows exactly where the crawler is weak. Fix it, run the test again, and you have a regression test for the crawler.

04 · Getting to 80%+

The crawler is one source out of seven

No crawler reads a backend. On its own it caps at about 20–30% of a working product. Combined with six public sources it reaches roughly 80–85%, and regulated sectors are easiest because law and standards publish most of their logic.

LayerCrawler aloneTargetHow
Colors, fonts, spacing95%99%Crawl every breakpoint (mobile, tablet, desktop). The browser reports the exact values.
Layout90%97%Also the mobile app: Android emulator + Appium dumps each screen's layout. Most Indian sector apps are app-first.
Visible features70–85%90%+Help centers (which document nearly every feature), app-store "What's new" notes, release notes, YouTube tutorials, vendor sales demos for admin/staff sides you can't sign up for.
Tech stack60–80%90%Job postings name the exact stack. Also engineering blogs, conference talks, Wappalyzer / BuiltWith, company GitHub.
API shape30–50%80%+Run every L2 workflow with network capture so every endpoint fires; add public developer docs (e.g. Zerodha Kite Connect is fully documented).
Backend logic0–10%70–85%Sector standards + open-source equivalents (§03) + black-box testing + patents + expert interviews.
Infrastructure5%70%Engineering blogs, AWS/GCP customer case studies, status pages, job postings.

Standards that define the backend, by sector

Hospitals

ABDM (national health stack, public APIs) · HL7 FHIR (patient-record format) · ICD-10 (diagnosis codes) · NABH (accreditation)

Labs

LOINC (test codes) · NABL (accreditation) · HL7 (instrument interfaces)

Stocks

SEBI regulations (margin, KYC, risk) · T+1 settlement · NSE/BSE specs · public broker APIs (Kite Connect, Upstox, Angel One)

05 · The critical correction

The crawler cannot see manual work

Consumer apps are the automated part of a company. The 30-minute human work (reconciliation, pathologist review, claims, KYC exceptions) happens inside, on internal tools no crawler reaches. The crawler maps a workflow's shape; these three sources show where the human effort sits.

Strongest signal

Job postings

Hundreds of companies hiring "medical coder", "claims processor", "KYC executive" or "reconciliation executive" means that work is manual. The number of openings suggests the volume, and the salary tells you what automating it is worth.

Ground truth

5–10 interviews

A clinic front desk, a lab technician, an accounts clerk, a broker's back office. They will tell you what they copy, re-type, chase and double-check. Your future clients can be these sources.

Where workflows live

B2B tools staff use

Hospital and lab management systems, accounting and service-desk software. Many have free trials, demos and detailed docs. Research the business side, not the patient app.

06 · The method

Nine questions for every workflow step

Ask these of each step of a mapped workflow. Every "yes" is a candidate opportunity.

1Does it require manual data entry?
2Does it require calling someone?
3Does it require checking multiple systems?
4Does it require reading PDFs or images?
5Does it require copying information across?
6Does it wait on an approval?
7Does it cause delays?
8Does it create errors, and what does an error cost?
9Does it happen thousands of times a month?

Example chain: hospital

Patient→Appointment→Doctor→Prescription→Lab order→Report→Insurance→Billing

Red steps: where the nine questions usually return "yes". Verify with interviews, don't assume.

08 · Scoring

Rank in rupees, then filter

Primary score: volume / month × minutes per task × staff cost per minute = ₹ saved per month. That number is your price anchor and your pitch.

Then filter the ranked list. Any "no" drops or delays the opportunity:

FilterPass ifWhy it matters
Frequency1,000+ times / month per clientRare tasks don't justify a product.
Digital inputsThe data already exists as text, PDF, image or a system recordPaper-only workflows need digitisation first.
Error costA mistake costs money, time or trustMakes accuracy a selling point, not a nice-to-have.
Human stays in controlAI drafts, a person approvesRequired in health and finance, and it makes the sale easier.
Buyer speedThe owner or one manager can say yesDecides whether you're paid in weeks or in a year (§09).
Regulatory burdenYou can meet it as a small teamBanks need audits and certifications before you start.

10 · Where to start

One sector first. Start where buyers decide fast.

Start here

Diagnostic labs or clinics

The owner often decides alone, in weeks. Structured, high-volume workflows. Open-source ground truth exists (OpenELIS, Bahmni). The public standards (LOINC, NABL, ABDM) spell out much of the backend.

Second

SMB accounting or enterprise service desk

Fast buyers, ERPNext and Zammad as ground truth, and reconciliation and ticket triage are classic 30-minutes-to-30-seconds work.

Later, with references

Banks, insurance, government

6–18 month procurement, vendor audits, often ISO 27001 or SOC 2 required. Bank apps are also the hardest to crawl, and automating your own bank login can lock your real account.

The three "research labs" (healthcare, financial ops, enterprise ops) are right for building breadth after the first paid pilot. Before that, three sectors at once means three unfinished funnels.
11 · First sector

A six-week cadence

A suggested pace, not a promise. Each week ends with a countable output; if it's missing, the week isn't done.

Week 1 · Ground truth Phase 0

Run the sector's open-source system locally, crawl it, rebuild the workflow maps from the crawl, compare with code and docs.

Done when: crawler accuracy % per layer is written down.

Week 2 · Map the sector crawler + docs

3 consumer apps + 3–5 B2B vendors. L1–L5 per product, one model session per product. Help centers, demos, release notes.

Done when: every core workflow is recorded step by step (L2).

Week 3 · Find the manual work job posts + interviews

Pull job postings for the sector's operational roles. Interview 5–10 people who do the work. Run the nine questions on every step.

Done when: a list of manual tasks, each with volume and minutes.

Week 4 · Price and rank §08

₹ saved per month for every task. Apply the filters. Pick the top 3.

Done when: 3 opportunity cards, each with a rupee number.

Week 5 · Sell before building 5 conversations

Show each prospect the number: "this costs you ≈ ₹X a month; would you pay ₹Y to remove it?" Adjust with what you learn.

Done when: at least one prospect agrees to a paid pilot.

Week 6 · Spec and build pilot

Product spec → architecture → build plan for the one workflow the pilot client will pay for. Own brand, own UI, own backend.

Done when: the pilot spec is signed off by the client.
12 · Tooling

What already exists, and what gets added

Already built · reuse, never rebuild

The competitor research engine

Login: save-session-cdp.js (you launch Chrome yourself, Playwright attaches; Google login works). Crawl: crawl.js produces L1–L5 per product. Only one config file per competitor changes between projects. Details: docs/L5-RESEARCH-KIT.md.

Five layers per product

L1 → L5

L1 screen map · L2 workflows step by step (the most valuable, and the one to fill first) · L3 network calls · L4 templates and presets · L5 conclusions.

To add

Mobile app mapping

Android emulator + Appium to dump app screen layouts, the mobile version of L1. Needed because most Indian sector products are app-first.

To add

Job-posting and interview log

One sheet per sector: role, number of openings, salary range, tasks named in the posting, interview notes. This is where manual work gets counted.

Budget rule: one product per model session. The model reads the kit and the outputs, never the crawler source; reading the source is what burns a session in ten minutes.

13 · Boundaries

What's allowed, as facts

Allowed

Studying workflows, features, ideas and common layout patterns · designing your own backend from observed behaviour · signing up for the same third-party vendors (payments, maps, SMS) with your own account · public business listings to find clients · personal data a person published themselves (DPDP Act §3(c)(ii)).

Not allowed

Their names, logos, text, images, icons, code or combined look (trade dress) · calling their private endpoints or using their keys · patient or health records, ever · personal data someone else posted about a person · phone numbers used for calls/SMS without TRAI DND compliance.

Known risks you've accepted: most sites' terms ban automated access, so expect possible account bans. Banks can lock a real account when a login is automated. The DPDP Act's main provisions take effect in phases through 13 May 2027. This is not legal advice.
14 · Honest ceilings

80–85% is reachable. 100% isn't, and isn't needed.

Why 100% is impossible

You can't trigger every state · matching outputs don't prove matching logic · their data shapes their results · behaviour at scale is invisible to one account · they ship weekly and run A/B tests, so the target moves.

What to aim for instead

Near-100% on the core workflows users spend their time in. "Same output" becomes provable through acceptance tests: this input → this output, this flow in this many steps. A build that passes the same tests counts as the same, whatever its code.

All percentages in this plan are estimates, not measurements. Phase 0 (§03) turns them into measured numbers for each sector.

# Section patterns

Building blocks for composing pages. Each is a structure, not a look — the genome
decides how it looks. Pick by the job the section has to do.

## Contents
Navigation · Above the fold · Explaining the product · Proof · Comparison and pricing ·
Conversion · Content · Company · Footer

---

## Navigation
- **Minimal bar** — logo, 3–5 links, one action. Most sites.
- **Split around logo** — links either side of a centred mark. Hospitality, fashion, editorial.
- **Mega menu** — grouped panels with short descriptions. Sites with 20+ destinations.
- **Persistent sidebar** — vertical navigation always visible. Docs, apps, reports.
- **Full-screen overlay** — menu takes the screen. Portfolios, studios.
- **Bottom tabs (mobile)** — app-like navigation for 3–5 core destinations.
- **Sticky progress** — bar shows reading or step progress. Long stories, onboarding.
- **Command-first** — search/command palette as the main way around. Developer tools.

## Above the fold
The `hero` axis of the genome names the type. Whatever the type, it must answer: what is
this, who is it for, what do I do next. One primary action; at most one secondary.

## Explaining the product
- **How it works** — 3–5 numbered steps, each a verb and an outcome.
- **Feature by job** — group features under the user's goals.
- **Product in code** — a real, interactive slice of the product UI (not a screenshot).
- **Annotated UI** — the product view with callouts explaining key parts.
- **Before / after** — the work without and with the product; side-by-side or slider.
- **Use-case tabs** — one tab per audience or scenario, each with its own example.
- **Scroll story** — the product changes state as the visitor scrolls.
- **Calculator** — the visitor enters their numbers and sees their result; show the formula.
- **Integrations** — only tools that genuinely connect; link to how.

## Proof (only what exists)
- **Customer story** — named customer, permission, measured result with its source.
- **Quotes** — real people, real names and roles, with permission.
- **Logos** — only customers who agreed to be shown.
- **Numbers** — real figures with the period and source ("1,240 bookings in March 2026").
- **Credentials** — regulators, certifications, audits — with registration numbers where relevant.
- **Security & privacy** — what data is kept, where, for how long, who can access it.
- **Process transparency** — how the work is done; works when there are no testimonials yet.

## Comparison and pricing
- **Plan cards** — 2–4 plans, the recommended one marked for a reason.
- **Comparison table** — full feature matrix below the cards; sticky header.
- **Usage pricing** — a slider or inputs that compute the price.
- **Versus your current way** — compare against the manual process, not named competitors.
- **FAQ** — the real questions sales hears; answers in two or three sentences.

## Conversion
- **Final call to action** — repeat the main action with a reason to act now (true ones only).
- **Inline form** — short form inside the page (email, quote request).
- **Booking** — date/time picker with availability.
- **Waitlist** — single field, a clear promise of what happens next.

## Content
- **Article grid** — cards with title, date, reading time.
- **Featured article** — one large story with an editorial layout.
- **Changelog** — dated entries, grouped by version.
- **Resource library** — filters by type and topic.

## Company
- **Mission** — one paragraph that a customer would recognise as true.
- **Team** — real people with roles; or no team section.
- **Timeline** — dated milestones that happened.
- **Locations** — offices or service areas with addresses.
- **Careers teaser** — open roles count and a link.

## Footer
Real contact details, the main routes, legal links, social links that exist, regulatory
information where the industry needs it (licence numbers, registered office), language switch
if the site is multilingual.
